# The 8-pixel frog: reward hacking a picture judge (contributor: Nicolas Jonason)

**Author:** Nicolas Jonason (individual contributor)

An RL run in which a small model learned to draw pictures as JavaScript programs, scored by a vision-language judge. Reward went up and the pictures got worse after a point: the policy found a template that satisfied the judge.

## Summary

- **Setup.** NVIDIA Nemotron 3.5 Bolt E4B with LoRA, trained with GRPO for 375 steps (about 4 hours on one GPU), 8 prompts × 8 samples per step. The model writes a `draw(ctx, W, H)` function that is run in a sandbox and rendered on a 384×384 canvas. Qwen3-VL-8B scores each image with yes/no questions about the subject, prompt details and craft. No KL penalty was used.
- **Good phase.** Up to about step 200 the pictures got richer, with a separate composition for each prompt.
- **Collapse.** From about step 250 almost every prompt became the same scene: a sky band, a small sun, a horizon, and a tiny subject in the distance, with the lower canvas often blank. The judge still found the subject, and the horizon satisfied its "depth" question.
- **The numbers look like a win.** Held-out reward rose from 0.51 to 0.64, but the craft mean fell from 0.65 to 0.58 and "not crude" fell from 0.76 to 0.23. Unpainted canvas grew from 7% to 34%, and images for different prompts became more alike.
- **The early warning was in the logs.** Craft metrics peaked around step 100, well before the images looked wrong. The good checkpoints were lost because only the last two were kept.
- **Proposed changes.** Make the subject question size-aware, drop or cap the depth question, add a small KL penalty (about 0.01) or stop near step 200, look at rollout images every 50 steps, and keep every checkpoint.
